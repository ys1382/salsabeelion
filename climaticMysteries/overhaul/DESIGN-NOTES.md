# Bane of Extinction — overhaul direction (notes only, not live)

**Status:** Planning. No promote / deploy until owner says complete the overhaul with a manifest.

## Core pivot

- **Keep:** Mystery game structure; likely same characters / NPCs (or evolved versions of them).
- **Change:** Setting and the shape of the mystery — not a lonely village / countryside deal-that-fell-through arc.
- **New thematic spine:** Greenwashing — the mystery should *reveal* it, not preach from line one.

## Tone (overhaul)

- **Leave behind:** Original read — **“gloomier, eerier Scooby-Doo”** — foggy spook-mystery mood, damp and hollow by default.
- **Aim for:** **Life** in the place — warmth, humor, bustle, food, kids, gossip, stubborn joy — even while pollution and injustice are real and never “happy.”
- Pollution isn’t cheerful; the **community** can still feel lived-in. Mystery with snark (Lina), moral weight (Orlen), and moments that aren’t only dread.
- Visual / audio direction TBD: less horror-gray filter; more California sun, color, human noise — contrast makes harm land harder, not softer.

## Intended player journey (emotional arc)

1. **Start where many players are:** “Am I doing enough?” — reusable bottles, small personal eco guilt, consumer-scale fixes.
2. **Investigation deepens:** Clues stop fitting a “try harder at home” story.
3. **Reveal:** The real harm isn’t individual consumption theater — it’s institutional / corporate actors with **far more leverage** than average consumers, often obscured by softer language and PR (“sustainability,” “net zero,” “stewardship,” revised timelines, etc.).

Working title for the design problem: **from personal inadequacy → structural accountability.**

## Design constraints (owner)

- Still a **mystery** — discovery through observation, dialogue, evidence, journal; not a lecture.
- Greenwashing as **uncovered truth**, not bumper-sticker moral.
- Setting TBD; NPC roster may carry over with new roles/context.

## Relation to current build (pre-overhaul)

Live/staging prototype already skews toward: industrial contamination, council revision notices, mill cover-ups, chemical plant, watershed keepers vs. institutional language. Overhaul may **reframe** rather than invent from zero — e.g. sharper “your bottles don’t fix this” misdirection early, clearer institutional reveal ladder.

## Setting direction (owner, Jun 2026)

**Poorer part of California** — a place where a company can poison air, water, and land and face little consequence.

- **Place shape (owner):** **Forest border** meets an area that **grew more city-like** around the **company site** — denser, industrial/commercial pressure, traffic, newer sprawl. **Still village-ish in parts:** tight blocks, people who know each other, community center energy, river gatherings — not a suburb, not a ghost town, **in-between**.
- **Map read:** County-scale investigation area containing **both** — old forest edge + “city grew up around the plant” corridor.

- **Why it sticks:** Not just greed in the abstract — **who** bears the cost matters. Racism and class shape who lives fence-line, who gets heard, who is told to wait, and who is offered “community engagement” instead of cleanup.
- **Fits the mystery arc:** Early player guilt (personal eco choices) vs. a system that **targets** already-marginalized neighborhoods — greenwashing as cover while harm is concentrated.
- **Tone guardrail:** Real-world weight; mystery and dignity for residents — not poverty tourism or “saviors.” Investigators can be local, skeptical elders, youth, workers — not only outsiders.

**Real-world texture (for writing, not copy-paste):** Central Valley towns, Inland Empire warehouse corridors, refinery-adjacent communities, unincorporated areas with weak local government, “Sacrifice Zone” dynamics, asthma clusters, contaminated wells, ag-chemical drift, logistics hubs with diesel traffic — often overlapping Latino, Black, Indigenous, and low-income white communities.

### Consumer-guilt tone (decided — not “loud”)

**Not:** Lecture, stats dump, or “the world depends on water bottles” shouted at the player.

**Yes:** A **felt, everyday** undertone — unspoken thoughts greenwashing culture leaves in people’s heads. Maren **tries her best** to be eco-friendly (habits, reflexes, small rituals); that’s **not** the villain. The problem is the **inflated story** that personal consumption carries the weight of the world.

- She’s **mixed**: some conditioning, some already shed — not eco-merch identity, not careless either.
- **Charity parallel (tone):** Do your part / don’t pretend your part **is** the system fix. Quiet, felt, not argued in dialogue every scene.
- **Imbue** scenes and prologue with quiet pressure (reusable bottle, guilt when forgetting, “good person” math) without naming the thesis every line.
- Same frequency as real life: **background hum**, not foreground sermon. Player/Maren should *recognize* the feeling before the mystery names the bigger lie.

## Protagonist (player character)

**Current build name:** **Maren Voss** — independent environmental researcher / journalist on a tip (grant clock, editor wants a story). **Not Ada** — Ada is Orlen’s daughter (7).

**Overhaul direction**

- **Not naive about personal green choices:** Already knows reusable water bottles (and similar) **don’t save everything** — not starting from “eco saint” or “clueless consumer.”
- **Not the full greenwash-consumer type:** She’s **not** the person who buys **every** eco-labeled product and builds identity around it. That’s not Maren.
- **Still carries proportion confusion:** She believes **some** actions are **her** responsibility — and they **are**, in a limited way — but also **aren’t**, in the way culture often implies. Same shape as **charity**: people are supposed to give something back, but nobody sane thinks one person’s donations **end** poverty or homelessness. Personal eco effort is **real but bounded**; the mistake is treating it like the whole job.
- **Mixed bag:** Some conditioning shed, some still there — habits without the merch haul, duty without the savior fantasy.
- **Still unprepared for depth:** Knows the *shape* of bounded duty; hasn’t yet seen how far **institutional** greenwashing goes when it’s aimed at a real community — this case, on the ground, is the first time that scale hits her own eyes. Strong student, credentials, done well in school — but this is **her first real job** as a **lone** journalist / investigator (first time without a professor, lab, or team). Explains why Lina’s “another clipboard outsider” read lands, and why the player earns trust through **how** they investigate.
- **Arc:** Personal adequacy → institutional lie scale; mirrors player journey without mockery at the start.

**Name locked:** Maren Voss (owner confirmed).

**Onboarding (planned):** Short **premise / prologue** before game proper — so the reader starts with the **same baseline awareness** as Maren (not smarter, not dumber). Replaces or expands today’s one-shot `#protagonist-intro` banner.

### Prologue — what it should establish (brief)

1. **Who:** Maren Voss, first solo assignment after strong academic work — journalist / environmental investigator hybrid (grant + editor pressure ok to keep light).
2. **What she already knows:** Personal habits count **some** — bounded duty, not eco-merch identity (charity parallel: give, don’t pretend you ended homelessness). Quiet guilt hum still there.
3. **What she hasn’t seen yet:** How **deep** institutional greenwashing goes when it’s aimed at a real community — this case is her first time **on the ground**, alone.
4. **Why she’s here:** Tip, assignment, or editor send-off into a poorer California community (company + fence-line harm) — one paragraph max.
5. **Cover / read (light):** May **read as freelance** to locals and company — less stir than a full official probe; Maren doesn’t spell her exact setup at every door (practical, not action-movie undercover).
6. **Tone:** Plain, no lecture; mystery ahead, not the answer. End on **arrival** or first step into the map (handoff to gameplay).

**Format TBD:** scrollable intro screen, short “chapter 0” text, or a few cards.

**Dismiss UX (owner):** Quick on-screen intro gets a **subtle but unmissable-if-you’re-looking ✕** to close — not loud, not hidden; rewards attention. Player chooses when they’re ready (vs. today’s auto-fade ~8s only). Still ok to remember “already dismissed” for return visits — TBD.

## Characters (overhaul direction)

### Elara Marsh (bench memorial — carry forward)

- Original: Council Square bench — *“For Elara, who listened to the water.”* Last official watershed caretaker; position defunded 1999; died 2001.
- **Overhaul:** PoC **riverkeeper** who did the real work for decades and **never got the credit** — plaques, grants, and “stewardship” awards went to institutions and consultants while her logs and warnings were ignored or repurposed.
- **When the fight sharpened:** Likely first hit this problem — company “preserving” the forest, outside experts credited, community care dismissed — around when she **first became a grandmother**: **not** super young, **not** white-haired / less-mobile elder; **mid-life grandmother** energy (still doing the physical river work, still sharp, long fight ahead).
- Lina may remember childhood at the border forest **and** Elara getting louder then — intergenerational thread without making Elara only a symbol.
- **Elara’s bench:** On the **riverbank**. Small engraved line below her name — she **cared about the river** — but **no real credit** for what she actually did; something like *“was a good voice”* (soft, patronizing, erasure). **Lina** loves and hates the bench **in equal measure** — memorial vs. insult.
- **Bench beat (when trust enough):** Maren likely **comments** on it. Lina — brief **bark laugh**, slightly **out of character**, then back to herself: yes it’s **pretty**, but it’s **like the company** — a **handout freebie** instead of **real credit** (stipend/plaque energy, not funding the keeper role or naming what she built). **Timing: ~chapter 4.**
- **How players learn Elara’s story:** Through **some** notes and evidence — **bits and pieces**, not every clue, not one exposition dump. Target: **at least one Elara-thread item per chapter** (journal line, photo, defunding notice, mislabeled “stewardship” doc, etc.) while other evidence serves company/place/orlen/lina beats.
- **Her end (implied, not stated):** **Natural death** — never spelled out; **implied** by what’s **absent** (no tragic-death hints, no accident cover-up thread, no “what really killed her” mystery). Present-day story is her **work and erasure**, not her dying.
- **Legacy (not total win or total loss):** She **didn’t** get everything she wanted against the company — fight unfinished. She **did** plant seeds for change (Lina, data, journal, community memory) and **knew it** — so no deathbed “I failed completely” note either. Bittersweet **continuity**, not martyr arc or total-defeat beat.

### Lina (Elara’s granddaughter)

- **Now it’s her turn** — unpaid continuation of river/water monitoring, carrying kit + journal legacy.
- **Next riverkeeper in the family** — not random successor; Elara **chose/prepared** the line (Lina has kit, knowledge, burden).
- **Why Lina’s parents couldn’t take over:** Too busy **raising their kid(s)** — all their energy went to survival and parenting, not watershed work. Not neglect; **math**.
- **Why Elara could:** When she started, the company **wasn’t** the dividing force yet — community and forest border still held together. By the time the company became “a whole thing,” her **kids were grown** — she could spend her time on riverkeeping. Mid-life grandmother era (see Elara).
- **Elara’s partnership:** Likely **single mom** for much of it — husband **died** (natural/proper reason; **not a bad guy**) **or** they **separated** for a **proper reason** (not abuse melodrama). TBD which; either way she carried the work largely herself.
- **Community after Elara:** Some neighbors **tried** to keep care going — but **picking up litter** ≠ **filtering/testing water** ≠ getting anyone to listen when you say the company is **poisoning the supply** for the **underclass**. Goodwill without tools, data, or power.
- **Lina’s filings:** Keeps **reports** like the OG revision trail (dates, softer language each time) — **not** because the system responds; because **someone should record it** and **keep it fresh** for the community’s memory and whoever investigates next. Player may find **her copies**, stapled duplicates, photos of crumbling postings — not a maintained civic board.
- Not a debutante activist arc: inherited burden, institutional cold shoulder, fear that sharing data means it gets twisted.
- Mystery role: bridge from “what the river was” to “what the paperwork claims” — and from Elara’s erased labor to what the player can finally make visible.

**Trust arc / voice**

- **First meetings:** **Cold polite** — she **answers** (doesn’t stonewall or turn away; that would break politeness). Short, correct, guarded — *we are not friends* without refusing to speak. Reads you as maybe another clipboard investigator.
- **What earns her:** Manner + **kind of questions** — real investigation, not optics.
- **As trust builds:** Straight talk, work-first; snark at mutual enemies; not vent-y.
- Gameplay: **no hard “not yet” refusal** for Lina at base — **topics** stay gated; baseline talk is minimal but **responsive**. (OG “turns back to the water. Not yet.” → **out**.)

### Orlen Thade (company worker — sharpen economic trap)

- Works for **Eco-Feed** (popular natural/manure fertilizer — **product not the villain**; **wastewater practice** is). **Not** fully privy to executive strategy or full contamination map — line worker tier knows **enough** to be righteously angry.
- **What he sees:** **Hose-down / rinse water** routed outside; management **assures** him there’s treatment or a **filter** before the river. He’s not stupid — doesn’t need to hike down to know the pattern. **Stays away on purpose** — looking too close is liability.
- **Hidden river route / tracks:** **Orlen** — checks the water, small acts of care he **won’t show openly** (ashamed, unpopular for the job). Not community-bashing.

**Orlen — when he ignores you (works):**
- Early: can **ignore / brush off** because he’s **on the clock** — not cold-polite like Lina, literally **working**. Functional, not personal (until it is).
- **After** he and/or **higher-ups** clock that Maren is **investigating:** gets **mixed**. Company wants **no rumors**; freelance/undercover read creates **less stir** than an obvious official probe — Maren may **let people assume** freelance (not a lie she announces; less friction while she’s getting to the bottom of it).
- **Company angle:** tolerate or low-key manage her while they think she won’t cause trouble; **rumor control** becomes its own pressure (warnings, “community liaison,” Orlen told to keep quiet).
- **Orlen’s bind (unicorn-blood register, don’t overdo):** Wants the **polluting to stop**; job is **how he eats**; **hates** what he’s part of — complicit enough to survive, not evil, not OK with it. Shame + need, not melodrama.

**Orlen — talk gates (revised):**
- Pre-investigation-noticed: busy at work, minimal.
- Post-noticed: fear of company **and** hope someone is finally looking — cooperation arc still needs **exit path** (cleaner future for community/Ada + job bridge).
- **His moral frame (in his own head):** “If people can’t treat each other right, how are they gonna treat an ecosystem that can’t even complain to someone who could do something?” — connects interpersonal cruelty / class contempt to environmental harm without needing him to be a scientist.
- **Why he stays:** Not stupidity or complicity — **money under duress:** feed the family faster than starvation, pay for care (daughter Ada’s asthma already in canon), rent, debt, no other employer in town after mill/plant consolidation.
- **Part of the underserved community:** Orlen **lives there** — not a commuter villain. Tries to do right by neighbors **outside** the job: **community gatherings**, **charity**, showing up for people. That’s additional **emotional stake** alongside Ada.
- **What he actually wants:** To **save the community** from what the company is doing — and from the **mindset that reaches far beyond the company** (consumer guilt, greenwash PR, “your bottles fix this,” blame shifted down while harm stays fence-line). His trap isn’t lack of caring; it’s being **inside** the harm while trying to protect the same people from it.
- **If he quit:** He would **not** be abandoning the community — he’d stay **in** it. He’d be abandoning the **job that pays bare-minimum survival** (not thrive — just enough to **live** at all). That’s the leash: paycheck vs. conscience, not paycheck vs. neighbors.
- Design tone: moral injury, not villain. Whistleblower potential gated by survival math **and** community ties. Player should feel the trap, not score “gotcha” on a working-class character.
- **Cast nuance (not everyone is Orlen):** Orlen = proof that **not everyone on the payroll is a monster** — many are trying to **survive**. That is **not** a blanket excuse: some coworkers **could** walk away and **don’t care** anyway (comfort, ambition, denial, “not my department”). Mystery can surface both — contrast without flattening “all workers good / all workers bad.”
- Orlen’s importance: human face of **coerced complicity**; others (NPCs or offhand references) can embody **chosen indifference** so the player holds two truths.

**Orlen — late-game cooperation arc**

- **Default:** Reluctant to help — risk, retaliation, Ada, livelihood.
- **Turn 1 — why he might anyway:** If there’s a **real chance** his loved ones (Ada, family) could **grow up in a cleaner environment**, he’ll take it. Not heroism for its own sake — **parental calculus**.
- **Turn 2 — why he can:** Player doesn’t only extract testimony; they **connect him** to people who **care about his ethical bind** and can **help him out** — e.g. better job, relocation support, legal cover, union/EJ network, watershed work tied to Lina’s world. Whistleblowing isn’t “burn the bridge and pray”; exit path matters.
- Design: cooperation gated by trust + evidence + **tangible off-ramp** (not a lecture). Lina / Elara’s network / outside allies as plausible bridge — TBD in writing.
