## No man-eating — any people (owner hard rule)

- **No race or culture eats people** — not as default, not as “some of them do,” not as folklore punchline, not as villain shorthand. **Cannibalism is not real in this world’s history or present** for any sentient people.
- **No cannibalism** in player-facing story as **fact**. Not family-friendly; ties real-world language communities to harm in ways that are **unacceptable** even for a minority of “evil” members.
- **Evil individuals exist** — corruption, tolls, exploitation, violence, prejudice, misuse of venom or law — **without** anthropophagy or “man-eater” species labels.
- **Predator peoples** (e.g. griffins) may be **carnivores toward ordinary prey** in ecology; **never** human-eating as culture, habit, or joke. **Manticores** and every other sentient people: **same rule.**
- **Avoid:** “man-eating monster,” “craves human flesh,” tribal cannibal tropes, or associating **Turkish, Arabic, Japanese, Irish**, or any real language group with cannibal imagery — including inverted “not all of them” exceptions.

### Allowed reference — false accusation vs. distorted history

**Man-eating — false rumor (never happened)**

- Bigots may claim **legendary dragons** or **dragonfolk** “ate humans long ago.” **Did not happen.** Flat denial; named prejudice.

**Hero vs dragon / hydra — may have happened; racism is the point**

- **Hercules-type legends** may reflect **real violence** in the deep past — a **human** (or ordinary mortal champion, **no deity**, no divine-judgment framing) fought dragons, wyverns, or **hydra-born** dragon children.
- **Why it happened:** **anti-dragon racism** and human propaganda — fear, scapegoating, land grabs, or myth-building — **not** because dragons were man-eaters or deserved extinction. The fight occurred **because** prejudice made conflict possible; the legend **celebrates the wrong side** in human retellings.
- **In-scene use:** dragonfolk or allies may say so plainly — **it happened, and it was wrong**; humans rewrote it as glory. Player learns **context**, not “dragons were the monsters.” **Never** replay as triumphant hero quest; **never** sacred or theological cosplay.
- **Hydra note:** if the tale targets a **multi-headed dragon child**, human stories **monsterize** a **rare birth** dragons raised with love — double layer of distortion.
- **Never** narrate man-eating as true; **never** use Hercules beats for gore sport or family-unfriendly detail.

**General rule for both**

- In-scene response from dragon-adjacent people: **outrage where appropriate**, **prejudice named**, not debated as “maybe dragons had it coming.”
- Keep **family-friendly**: plain speech, no graphic flashbacks, no “dark lore” relish. Myth names are **reference shorthand**, not IP imitation.

## Manticores (present-day people)

**Not** the cruel monster default. **Sentient, majestic, benevolent-by-default** — individuals can still be villains from corruption or circumstance, **never** from man-eating or cannibal tropes.

### Culture / language

- **Turkic speakers** — Turkish at high level among related Turkic tongues; not Spanish-default at home.
- **Bridge Spanish** where trade routes need it; dignity in speech; hospitality and route-oath traditions (details TBD).
- **Avoid:** monster-of-the-week framing, “evil beast” shorthand, Islamicate or Turkic **stereotype** dress-up.

### Form (readable, not random)

- Classic **lion** heads exist; not required for everyone.
- Lineages vary: **lioness** heads; **snow-leopard** faces with **lion mane**; other feline mixes that read **clear and consistent**, not novelty shape-shifting.
- **Scorpion tail** (when present): tool, balance, signal — **not** constant poison-gore spectacle.
- **No man-eating** — see global rule above. Not defined by eating people; not “reformed man-eaters.”

### Jobs / vibes

- Pass guardians, storm readers on high trails, caravan escorts, storytellers of old routes, teachers of mountain etiquette.
- **Majestic** body language — warn, guide, judge fairly, rescue — not performative cruelty.
- Diet and biology stay **ordinary-animal** if carnivore; **never** people as food. Culture emphasizes oaths, hospitality, and community trust.

### Story

- Environmental, detective, and supply-chain threads can cross manticore passes without combat-as-default.
- Attunement may surface them for players who leaned **land/routes/detail** pre-train — not exclusive.

## Dragons, wyverns, and hydras (legendary tier)

### Dragons and wyverns

- **Legendary / unreachable** in present-day street story — believed **long gone**; may still exist somewhere.
- **Dragonfolk** descend from this line — present-day people, not “dragons on the sidewalk.”
- Dragons **treat their children well** — family care is part of legendary culture; use for dragonfolk echoes (dignity, memory, formal care), not “abandoned wyrmling” clichés.

### Hydras (rare dragon variation)

- **Hydras may exist** — not a separate “village-raiding monster” species. In this world they are best read as **dragon conjoined twins, triplets, or similar**: **one dragon body, multiple heads** from the same birth, not a stack of disposable heads that regrow for combat set-pieces (avoid gamey hydra tropes unless owner revisits later).
- **Uncommon** — like conjoined twins in any people, **not the norm**; happens **sometimes**, not never.
- **Taken in stride** among dragons: **loving, practical family response** — raised as **their child**, not a curse, sideshow, or prophecy omen. Other dragons don’t gawk; it’s **rare but ordinary within dragon life**.
- **Present-day story:** same as dragons — **legendary**, mostly **off-screen**; human folk tales may **monsterize** hydra births or fold them into **Hercules**-type “hero” stories. Those tales may **remember real violence** — but **anti-dragon racism** caused it; dragons were **not** man-eaters; see distorted-history rule.
- **Avoid:** “each head is evil,” disposable head regeneration as gore sport, hydra as default enemy type, or framing multi-headed dragon children as abominations.
