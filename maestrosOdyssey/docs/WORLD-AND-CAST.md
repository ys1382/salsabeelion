# Maestro’s Odyssey — world and cast

**Status:** Owner **cast bible** — peoples, jobs, languages, story shape. Live `www/` may lag until owner asks to ship aligned story.

**See also:** `LOREBOOK.md` (hard rules: no man-eating, distorted dragon history, manticores/sphinx/dragons detail), `DRAGONS-BREW-PRE-TRAIN.md` (committed café week-one plot), `CONTRIBUTOR-GUIDELINES.txt` (tone gates, spine summary).

---

## Setting frame

**Storybook near-present** city world: cafés, schools, stations, markets — not medieval-only; phones and cars are not the early vibe.

**Magical realism:** species are **cultures with jobs**, not enemy races. **No theology creatures**, no stock RPG stereotypes.

**Legendary tier:** **Dragons and wyverns** are believed long gone (may still exist somewhere) — **not** everyday commuters. Present-day **dragonfolk** descend from that line.

**Tone reference (not copy):** warm, grounded quest feeling — family-friendly, not goofy; not chosen-one.

---

## Story shape (not chosen-one)

The world was already running. The protagonist belongs here. Ordinary mornings intersect situations already in motion — they **noticed**, **cared**, or **happened to be useful**, and a thread pulled.

### Spine + body

- **Fixed spine, variable body** — structural idea only (everyone gets the same opening beats; later paths diverge).
- **Dragon's Brew first** — not a wake-up menu choice. Every run: thermos, barista, first Spanish-in-context beat (e.g. *azúcar*), neighborhood familiarity.
- **Plaza floor required** — mercado, public square, market-under-tracks. Everyone gets **tourist-ready getting-around** Spanish: food, bag, price, hours, thanks — not fluent, not performing culture.
- **Neighborhood phase (pre-train):** block-bound (café + plaza + tied errands) until basics done — same life everyone has before riding out without getting lost. **Dragon’s Brew** is the social crossroads (mixed species, overheard lore); **no** full grocery / general-store loop before the train — see `DRAGONS-BREW-PRE-TRAIN.md`.
- **Train = wider world:** area independence opens — choose destinations, other registers and tongues ahead — **competence gate**, not prophecy.
- **Body after train:** routes and inclinations diverge — supply-chain curiosity, environmental (merfolk/coastal), detective (dragonfolk intrigue), gardener (giants/land-care), etc. **Different paths, same anchored start.**

### Attunement

- Pre-train **activity choices** (produce labels, truck unload, planters, sea-adjacent errands, puzzle/detail) **surface what you might like first** later — gardening lean → giants/planting hooks rise on the list; detective and merfolk paths stay available.
- **First on the list**, not **only path**. **Pivot anytime** — attunement follows **current** behavior; no permanent class from early play.

### Supply-chain storytelling (anti-monotony)

Before big arcs land, **mundane cart, fantastical supply chain underneath**:

- Milk from **minotaur dairies**; fruit from **giant orchards** and/or **humid-belt small growers** — learned through labels, delivery chatter, crate stamps, not lore pop-ups.
- Same location, different layer each visit: chalk price crossed out, supplier switched, cooperative hours changed — world moves off-screen; player catches up.
- One glance deep per visit — overheard line, sign detail — not a logistics lecture.

### Player Spanish bar

- **Survival / getting-around** — infer from context, small confident phrases, read signs and menus.
- **Not** politician, diplomat, culture teacher, or “I will lecture you on customs.”
- Formal dragonfolk or maritime registers are **other people's houses**; player earns entry, not authority.

### Language in play

Language tied to **survival, travel, trust, customs, conflict avoidance** — not a gimmick.

**Protective inference:** learn to **recognize and avoid** alcohol words (*cerveza*, *vino*, *licor*, labels, bar context) through the same context-clue skill as everyday vocab — game **heavily discourages** alcohol; see `mo-avoidance-vocab.js` when wired.

## Languages across peoples (owner intent)

- **Early player slice:** Spanish is the **target language** in the protagonist’s everyday city — café, corner store, mercado, train gate. **Not** “everyone in the world speaks Spanish.”
- **Community tongues:** Aside from peoples **already heavy in Spanish contact** (dragonfolk, **aquatic/coastal peoples**, goblin/smallfolk traders, centaurs on Spanish-facing routes), most cultures use **their own main language(s)** at home. **Which tongue names what** is still open.
- **Bridge speakers (for now):** In **each** people, **at least one or two individuals** speak **Spanish well** — real conversation, not tourist phrases. Majority may not; early scenes run through bridges until player earns wider access.
- **Later regions:** Irish, Japanese, Arabic, Turkish (and others) unlock with geography and trust — see `LOREBOOK.md`.
- **Pegasi / Griffins:** Human **spoken** Spanish is not the default (anatomy). “Spanish well” = **literacy**, sign/sky-sign fluency, or **trusted interpreter** — not every winged person speaking aloud.

**Spanish-heavy (but not Spanish-only at home):** dragonfolk, aquatic/coastal peoples, goblin/smallfolk traders, Spanish-route centaurs.

**Fluency ≠ friendly** — especially aquatic peoples: maritime Spanish for permits and harm-reading; still may refuse human business.

## Dragonfolk *(present-day)*

Descended from dragons/wyverns — not “dragons on the street.”

**Vibe:** Old dignity, quiet strength, deep memory, formal and observant.

**Jobs:** Guides, mentors, scribes, teachers, archivists.

**Morality:** Can be villains, oppressors, invaders — **not all evil**.

**Body:** Warm-blooded, heat-tolerant, scales/horns, strong smell sense; some fire/smoke/wing remnants; strong but not giant-killers.

**Languages:** **More than bilingual** — Spanish and English **both** at high level among other tongues for many in public-facing roles. **Among themselves**, dragonfolk spaces may default to **archival or elder tongues**, not Spanish. Default register with learners is **formal** (*¿Te gustaría…?*, complete sentences) — not cruel, but **not** café *¿Quieres…?* Player earns trust with a **small, confident word set**, not fumbling big vocabulary. Above the “one or two bridges” bar — still not Spanish-only at home.

**History (see LOREBOOK):** anti-dragon “hero” legends may remember real violence; man-eating smears are **false**.

## Lizardfolk *(distinct from dragonfolk)*

Ecology and adaptation — not draconic blood.

**Body-care culture:** Spas, scale salons, shedding annoyances.

**Lineages (examples):** desert sand-swimmers and quicksand rescue; sea salt-tolerant sailors; color-shifters (art, dye, escape work); venomous lineages in **medicine/antivenom** — **who’s venomous is private**; skink/chameleon-like (lookouts, trackers, babysitters, undercover).

**Brumation:** Slow periods — possible clues to old disappearances.

**Still TBD:** gecko pads, tail drop, burrowing, etc.

**Languages:** **Own tongue(s)** by lineage and habitat — not Spanish as community default. **At least one or two** Spanish speakers per enclave for now (spa reception on a trade route, salt-cargo clerk); names TBD.

## LavaBorn / firefolk

Heat and fire tied to **craft**, not warrior class.

**Jobs:** Bakers, cooks, glassblowers, potters, candle makers, lantern keepers, fire-safety teachers, festival vendors, winter shelter hosts, smiths (among others).

**By age:** Kids = no jobs; teens = safe vendor practice; elders = careful flame roles.

**Languages:** **Own tongue(s)** around kiln and neighborhood craft — not Spanish at home for most. **At least one or two** Spanish speakers per community for now (festival vendor facing the city, fire-safety teacher on shared contract); tongues TBD.

---

## Desert / dune dwellers

Generational land experts — **the land is a language**.

**Jobs:** Storm readers, caravan planners, salt/sand merchants, map correctors, track readers, signal watchers, desert medics.

**Skill:** Early sandstorm reads, hidden water, old wells, shade and wind routes.

**Languages:** **Desert tongues** first — land-as-language is not Spanish. **At least one or two** Spanish speakers per caravan or signal post for now (storm reader on city contract, salt merchant at border stall); names TBD.

## Minotaurs

**Always vegetarian** — every role.

**Jobs:** Dairy, milk merchants, cold-weather bovine care; wool/fiber by **shearing/brushing** (never slaughter); warehouses, routes, maps, heavy lifting.

**Vibe:** Patient, direction-minded (maze legend as flavor, not joke).

**Villains:** Corruption, tolls, route control, hoarded maps — **not** carnivore brutality or meat-eating.

**Languages:** **Bovine languages** plus **herd/collective tongue(s)** — not Spanish as farm default. **At least one or two** Spanish speakers per cooperative (city milk liaison, route map clerk at mercado) — supply-chain scenes like grocer stock talk.

---

## Crocodilian riverfolk

Marsh and river specialists; **breathe air** (not amphibious).

**Jobs:** Flood rescue, river patrol, canal guards, ferry escorts, bodyguards, trackers.

**Vibe:** Disciplined, quiet, formidable — **respected**, not “savage.” Reputation **protects** places tied to them.

**Core:** Not aggressive by default — crossing them **in their territory** is the mistake.

**Languages:** **River and marsh tongues** — not Spanish as territory default. **At least one or two** Spanish speakers per patrol or ferry house (canal-guard translator, flood-rescue clinic contact); names TBD.

---

## Centaurs

Road people — **couriers and messengers** at the heart.

**Jobs:** Messengers, equinoid vets, hoof care, pack/travel advisors.

**Relations:** Strong ties to **Pegasi**; helped push **animal-welfare laws** for horses/equinoids; **never** treat Pegasi as mounts.

**Story role (early):** Optional **first guide** — warm ambassador-courier, not mentor or chosen-one teacher.

**Languages:** Own tongue + **one route language learned well** (Spanish, German, etc.) + a little of others — human-facing **ambassadors**, not all polyglots. Centaurs on Spanish-facing routes exceed the “one or two bridges” bar; **most centaurs are not** on those routes.

---

## Pegasi

Mountain / cloudland people — **sentient, not rideable**.

**Speech:** **Cannot speak** human languages (anatomy); read/write; sign, wing/hoof gesture, **sky-sign**.

**Vibe:** Proud, guarded, freedom-focused; herbivores; **wary of griffins**.

**Languages:** **Sky-sign and Pegasus gesture** at the roost — not human SL. Pegasi **can understand human sign language** when others use it but **do not sign it themselves**. In mixed company, a **griffin interpreter** often **signs SL on their behalf** or relays sky-sign into SL. **At least one or two** per roost **read and write Spanish well** for treaties and mountain–city contact. Full SL teaching/animation **TBD**.

## Griffins

Mountain/sky people — **beaks; no human speech**.

**Jobs:** Hunters, scouts, guides, camp protection, lost-traveler rescue.

**Relations:** Carnivores → tense with Pegasi; **negotiate as equals** — pay for **service**, not obedience. **Debt:** may save someone who cannot pay and ask for **humane work** later.

**Languages:** **Human-oriented sign language (SL)** is a griffin strength — especially **signing SL for Pegasi** in mixed company. Also **griffin mountain tongues** among themselves. **At least one or two** per eyrie **read/write Spanish** or interpret for hire; many scouts learn **phrase Spanish** for contracts only. Which SL and full lesson design **TBD**.

---

## Giants / troll-like beings

Large-scale workers and **land-sensitive guardians**.

**Jobs:** Stone, timber, roads, bridges, gates, cliffs; **orchards** at giant scale.

**Vibe:** Not clumsy oafs — delicate work is **inconvenient**; need space, wide paths, high ceilings.

**Core:** **Feel land changes** — cannot pretend the ecosystem is fine when it isn’t.

**Languages:** **Own tongue(s)** for road crews and orchard cooperatives — not Spanish at home for most. **At least one or two** Spanish speakers per work band (timber-yard city liaison, storm-warning poster at mercado); names TBD.

---

## Goblin / redcap-ish smallfolk

Forest, swamp, edge-of-town — **organized**, not comic savages.

**Vibe:** Small, quick, travel in groups; efficient **coordination**, not cruelty.

**Jobs:** Foragers, trappers, repair, scouts, guides, toolmakers — self-sufficient.

**Phrase:** Jack of all trades, masters of many.

**Guardrail:** No Native/religious stereotype framing, no “shaman” tropes.

**Languages:** **More than bilingual** — Spanish fluently among other tongues for many traders; **not** spoken at home. **Casual merchant energy** with outsiders — prefer own tongue in-group, **meet customers halfway** when practical. Fast, layered speech — not lesson Spanish, not dragonfolk formal. More Spanish-capable than “one or two bridges” — still not Spanish-only.

---

## Aquatic / coastal humanoids *(not generic merfolk)*

Deep-water and coastal — **modest designs**, not seductive stock merfolk.

**Jobs:** Pearl divers, fish-finders, reef/tide specialists.

**Politics:** **Anti-human** over trawlers, industry, ecological harm — usually **no business** with humans; **rare old friendships** with individual fishing families.

**Actions:** Cut illegal nets, sabotage harmful gear, protect turtle nests — protest/resistance, not war.

**Core:** Protective sea communities; humans **misrepresent** anger as “water gremlins” for profit.

**Languages:** **People of the sea** — seas stretch far; coasts meet many tongues. **More than bilingual** for many in coastal-contact roles: **Spanish at high level** among other human languages and **sea tongues of their own** — similar to dragonfolk breadth, in some ways **more** (ports, manifests, dialects). **Among themselves**, reef and deep-water communities default to **sea tongues**, not Spanish.

**Important:** Fluency does **not** mean warmth. Language skill serves **permits, trawler manifests, protest signage, rare negotiations, old friendships** — not tourism. Spanish with a human tends to be **precise and watchful** (names, dates, legal words, ecological terms) — courtroom-clear when the sea is at stake, not café casual.

**Player-facing:** Can understand a stumbling learner; respect from **listening carefully** and **not wasting words**. Spanish well is **necessary, not sufficient** for trust.

## Snakefolk

Slender-space specialists — **often misjudged** (famous villains skew the stereotype).

**Jobs:** Cave guides, mine/drain inspectors (not sewage), crawlspace work, archive shelf-runners (with dragonfolk), stage movement teachers, escape artists, **detectives**.

**Vibe:** Graceful, precise, ribbon-like — **not ritualistic**.

**Lore hint:** Possible distant tie to sky-serpents / dragon-wyvern lineages.

**Languages:** **Own tongue(s)** for crawlspace guilds — not Spanish default. **At least one or two** Spanish speakers per crew (court liaison, city-facing instructor); names TBD.

## Vampires *(present-day)*

People with dietary need for **blood**; sourcing is **tech / clinic** based (not fangs in public). **Vampire cafés** exist elsewhere — donation economy, ethical human blood allowed there; **not** on inclusive house menus like Dragon’s Brew. Individuals can be villains (corruption, mafia) — **not** a species default. See **`DRAGONS-BREW-PRE-TRAIN.md`** for week-one innocent bystander and mafia head thread.

## Werewolves *(present-day)*

People; **form** presentation varies by individual and situation. Some present in **werewolf form** in public when comfortable; others control **human-form** read more strongly (mechanism and why **TBD** per lineage). Family customs (e.g. chaperone for public evenings) are cultural, not predator trope. See **`DRAGONS-BREW-PRE-TRAIN.md`**.

- Player learns worlds like real cultures: transit, food, work, trust, register — not monster grinding.
