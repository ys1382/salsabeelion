## Live ship scope (owner — pinned)

**Plot, phases, and full design stay in this file** (source of truth in git).

### Main throughline (what the game is mostly about)

**Story + perception arc** — prologue, retreat contrast, Phase 3–5 emotional beats, small actions, restoration. **Drift-away** is the main **interactive** expression of “nature changed how they see the city” — zoning out at **work** (and elsewhere), retreat memory bleeding in, player clicks to refocus. **~3 drifts/day** in Phase 3, **~10 sec** each. Fast-forward (Day 2+) after Day 1 teaches the pattern.

### Ships on live — environmentally on-theme (not the main thing, not filler)

**Keepsake / bingo / sketchbook / journal** — **more directly tied to environmental themes** than laundry, dishes, or basket pack (species, natives/invasives, locale lists, Sheet A/B alternation). **Ships on live** when built: Phase 2 **scan capture**, Phase 3 **keepsake review** (favorites → flashbacks), Phase 4+ **research** from those favorites. Important for ecological literacy — **not** the spine of the game the way drift and story are. **Not** cut as domestic padding. Sheet text/layout can be locked early; scan + flashback playback are live build targets (unlike cut chore games).

### Cut from live (domestic filler — doc only)

**Laundry**, **dishes**, grocery **basket pack**, humane-society **socializer** click games, and similar step/score padding — cozy homework, **weak environmental tie**. Documented below; **do not ship**. **Live instead:** checklist + compass errands; shelter/home as **animation-led** beats + **drift**.

### Other live interactives

Job pick · retreat pick · checklist + compass grocery · light choices / fast-forward.

## Roadmap

### Phase 1 — Normal city life

The protagonist begins in a city-like environment. They have not yet realized anything is wrong.

**Target length:** Full Phase 1 playthrough (job pick → work beats → grocery → home → retreat discovery → booking/luggage) should land at **~2 minutes at most** — tight prologue, not a long tutorial.

**Mood (unchanged):** Everything feels normal; construction noise is ordinary; muted nature goes unnoticed; environmental imbalance is normalized. People still drive, shop, work, and live everyday life. **City is alive, not dead** — see **City vs nature design philosophy** (game-wide, from Phase 1).

#### Opening — job choice (before scenes)

Right before Phase 1 scenes play, the player picks one of **five** basic roles (Stardew-style “pick your animal” — flavor only, not the story focus). No alcohol-linked work or family-unfriendly jobs.

| **Author** | **Own day order** (see below) — café → grocery → home to write again → lands on a **nature-retreat ad** (see Author path). |

#### Humane society path (shelter shift → store → return rotation)

**No-kill shelter (owner — pinned):** The humane society where the protagonist volunteers is a **no-kill** shelter — animals are not euthanized for space. Tone stays **family-friendly**; plot stress comes from **care, capacity, adoption, rehab**, not kill-shelter beats.

**All indoors** — no nature-outdoors volunteer work. Real shelters use separate zones; this game uses simplified, family-friendly versions.

**Five-shift animal rotation (owner — on the calendar; not a vague post-trip callback):** One animal **zone/theme per shift**. Player does **not** see every type on shift 1; later shifts are **scheduled** on the Phase 3 / Phase 4 calendar.

**Plot role (owner):** **Every** humane-society animal shift is **plot-important** — not optional filler. Two story threads:

- **Mammals** (shifts **1–3** — dogs/puppies, cats/kittens, rabbits & guinea pigs/hamsters) → **domestic animals’ impact on nature** (pets, ferals, habitat pressure, responsible care — family-friendly beats; exact story beats TBD).
- **Birds + reptiles** (shift **4** — reptiles incl. **turtles** in rehab/observe; shift **5** — birds) → **native species** — local wildlife, rehab, ecology; ties to growing awareness and Phase 4 research beats where relevant.

Player goes to a **grocery store** to learn everyday city rhythm. **They don’t shop every day** — trips when staples run low (Phase 1 pre-trip; Phase 3 **Tuesday** restock after the retreat). **Lives alone:** keeps **light stock** at home, so runs skew **food-heavy** (fresh produce and bread go first).

- **Checklist** (Untitled Goose Game–style): gather items, then **compass to register**. **Common, everyday picks** — examples OK, not fixed SKUs:
  - **Fruit** (e.g. blueberries, apples — any common fruit)
  - **Vegetable** (e.g. cucumbers, carrots — any common vegetable)
  - **Bread**
  - **Soap**
  - **Laundry detergent**
  - **Disinfectant**
  - **Toothpaste**
  - **Floss**
  - **Author only:** **ink refill** (pen/printer — pick one in implementation)
- Purpose: teach how errands and the store **function** without making shopping the long-term focus. **Phase 1:** keep the run **tight** for ~2 min prologue (same list, minimal friction). **Phase 3 Tuesday:** same checklist + **drift** beats (see Phase 3 Day 2).

**Grocery (owner — live):** **Checklist + compass to register** only (Untitled Goose Game–style gather). **Full design (doc only):** **basket pack** minigame after all items found — cut as filler; not live.

- **Basket pack (fruit-clash / block-blast vibe):** Grocery pieces **fall**; player **arranges** them to **fit together** in a **small shopping basket** (tight footprint — Tetris-adjacent, not full Block Blast campaign). **Lose** if anything lands **outside the basket** → **retry** (quick restart, not a harsh penalty). **Tune:** engaging enough that it’s **not** a guaranteed one-try win every time, but **not** frustrating — short attempts, fair physics/readability, no rage-quit pacing.

**Protagonist habits (background, not lecture):** They carry a **reusable water bottle** and **usually avoid** plastic water bottles and food/drinks in **single-use containers** (shown through play, not a moral speech).

### Phase 2 — Visit to a more alive environment

The protagonist visits a place where nature is healthier and more vibrant — **contrast**, not **utopia** (**nature creates contrast**, **nature is not magic** under **City vs nature design philosophy**). Nature helps them feel what’s **missing** elsewhere; it is **not** a promise to abandon the city.

**Setting direction (owner):**

- **Avoid for Phase 2 open:** **Jungle** (too loud / saturated for this beat). **Restored natural area** (spoiler for later restoration).
- **Retreat choices (FTTB — three for now):** chosen in **Phase 1**, not Phase 2 — see **Retreat pick (prologue)**. **All California for now:**
  1. **Beach cottage** → **Newport Beach** — low-key coast; cottage scale, not boardwalk energy.
  2. **Cabin in the woods** → **Lake Tahoe** — timber, trail mud, porch; Sierra forest without jungle density.
  3. **Valley gorge + lakeside cabin** → **Yosemite *or* Russian River** (see **Gorge locale assignment** below) — alpine valley / gorge walls *or* river-redwoods gorge; cabin near water (dock or shore path, not a resort strip).

**Gorge locale assignment (owner):** Player picks option **3** (forest/gorge card) — they do **not** pick Yosemite vs Russian River. Game **assigns one automatically**, alternating: if the **previous player** who chose gorge got **Russian River**, the **next** gorge player gets **Yosemite**, and vice versa. Persist assignment (e.g. local flag or account) so the flip stays fair. Same beat skeleton; art, species lists, and copy swap per assigned gorge locale.

*Optional later (not FTTB):* **More retreat locations** as in-game choices after first trip — see **Your additions**.

*Other countryside ideas (meadow inn, farm-edge, etc.) parked until needed — not in the FTTB trio.*

*Reference only:* Real place names = **locale shorthand** for species, vibe, and art direction — not copying a real place shot-for-shot.

**This area feels:**

- Warmer
- Richer
- More alive
- Full of birdsong
- Layered with environmental sounds

**Looks / motion:** **More vibrant** color and animation than the city — but restrained enough to stay cozy and **not** foreshadow Phase 3’s muted return (see **Color / animation** under Creative direction).

This becomes the protagonist’s first experience with what environmental richness is supposed to feel like.

They may not fully understand why it feels different yet, but the contrast stays with them.

**Phase 2 uses the retreat they already picked** in the prologue (beach cottage / woods cabin / gorge lakeside cabin). Same beat skeleton; art, audio, and a few location beats swap per choice.

**Pacing (owner, FTTB):** Each **in-game day** ≈ **10 minutes real time** for now (tune later). **Departure morning** (pack, leave for city) ≈ **5 minutes real time**.

**Saves:** Player can save **any time** — right **after** completing an activity, including **morning wake-up** (checkpoint = activity done, not mid-action).

**Stay structure (owner) — not one shared three-day script.** Each retreat has its own day plan for **Days 1–3**.

**Arrival & leaving (shared across all retreats):** Same beats / same structure for **every** retreat — **only the scenery through the windows changes** (beach vs woods vs gorge). No separate arrival or departure scripts per location beyond art/audio swap.

**Shared arrival pattern (FTTB):** short **drive-up** (~1–1.5 min real time) → **settle** (e.g. book at cabin/cottage) → **sunset / early evening → village general store**.

**Shared leaving pattern (FTTB):** **~5 min real time** morning — pack / leave; window scenery matches chosen retreat; then return to city (Phase 3 handoff TBD).

#### Keepsake sheets — 24–25 cells, player alternation (owner)

**Design intent (owner):** Cover the **full species pool** (and **rocks/landforms** on gorge sheets) **across players** without **overwhelming one player**. Each playthrough shows **24–25 labels** — cozy, not a homework dump. **Sheet A + Sheet B** together hold the **complete set** for that retreat; alternation means repeat players eventually see **different** subjects in the same slots while any **one** run stays bounded.

### Phase 4 — Growing skepticism

**Phase 4 mindset (owner — pinned):** Compassionate **and** determined — **not** condescending toward others. The protagonist notices gaps between **what systems and crowds could do** vs **what actually happens**, and thinks: *I could do better here — why don’t they?* — while knowing **some people truly can’t** (time, money, health, caregiving). The story beat is: **enough** people **do** have slack that waste and disposables **shouldn’t** be at this **massive scale**. People should do **what they can, when they can** — different lanes for different lives (youth without kids can start with reusables and small habits; a parent with time might do leftovers; someone with a yard waters natives; etc.). **No** shaming players or NPCs who need plastic or packaged food that week.

**Determination without answers (owner — pinned):** They **don’t know** the fix — not for the city, not for the planet. But they **can’t stand** how things are. So they’re going to **try to help anyway**, knowing **picking up litter won’t save the world by itself, by themselves** — and doing it **anyway**. Facing environmental catastrophe with **resolve to do *something***, not a blueprint. Voice reference (end of **Phase 4 Day 1**, when processing first lands): *I don’t know what — but this can’t go on.*

**Greenwashing callout (owner — pinned):** Phase 4 names the **comfort lie** people hear — e.g. *you’ll save the world by tossing stuff in recycling* — and treats it as **greenwashing**, not gospel. Protagonist does small stuff **not** because they buy that pitch. Voice reference: *It’s going to take a LOT more work than I can do right here, right now.* The **why** behind acting (full): **better than sitting on my hands watching things get worse** — while staying **on the lookout for what will ACTUALLY cause change** (systems, scale, real leverage — Phase 4 plants the search; later phases carry it). **Not** cynical apathy; **not** recycling-bin savior fantasy. Full arc: **Greenwashing & system discovery**.

### Phase 5A — Small personal actions

Without fully knowing what else to do, the protagonist starts making small improvements almost on autopilot.

Not activism. Not saving the world. Just small acts of care in places they personally spend time.

**Realism guardrails (owner — pinned):** Only actions **busy, stretched schedules** can actually do — not “ideal green citizen” homework. Protagonist is **not** written as a parent; constraints are **human** (time, money, energy, setup), not a job type.

- **No** rinsing all plastic before recycling (time, water, soggy paper in mixed bins).
- **No** guilt about packaged food or plastic when that’s what feeds people that week.
- **Dishes / water (owner — pinned):** **Parents** washing after a **kids’ dinner** — **no** guilt beat on water use; feeding people comes first. **Kids** often have enough free time to **hand-wash before dishes pile up** — valid lane in the world’s “who can when.” **Students** (not **med-student**-level overloaded): often **smaller, simpler meals** for themselves — **hand-wash before pile-up** is **doable**; if a **working parent** sometimes manages dishes, a **student with a lighter plate** can too. Protagonist **lives alone** — Phase 5A treats **keep up before pile-up** as an **extension** habit when schedule allows, **not** a detergent ad or water lecture (no dishes minigame on live).
- **No** extra trips, LED preaching, or perfect-shopper framing.
- **Garden/plant beats:** **native or native-friendly** at minimum when planting or tending. **Can’t always trust seed swaps** — prefer labeled local natives, trusted ID, or keepsake-list reference; no blind swap-table invasives.
- **Compost, line-dry, bike, yard invasives, birdbath, decals:** only when the player **already has** the setup or is **already** in that context — offered from the pool, not forced defaults.

**Structure — 3 starters per place, then rotate in more**

Four **places:** **home**, **outdoor**, **work** (or school day), **elsewhere** (indoors, not home or work — grocery, café, thrift, drop-off).

Each place starts with **3 doable options**. When the player is **handling** those without it feeling like homework, **offer new ones** from that place’s **extension pool** (swap in or add — design TBD). Same cozy repeat-friendly loop, not a scorecard.

**Player alternation (owner — pinned):** Like **gorge Yosemite ↔ Russian River** and **keepsake Sheet A ↔ Sheet B**, eco-action **starter trios are not one fixed set for everyone**. Persist a flip (save / local flag): e.g. if the last player got **Outdoor starter set A**, the next gets **set B** (different 3 from the same place’s approved list). ### Phase 5B — First proof of impact

Over time, the protagonist notices subtle but real changes — **shown and heard**, not explained on a chart.

**Sensory payoff (owner — pinned):** The game’s job is to make **easy-to-miss real-life wins** **visible in the moment** — grass that **literally gains warmth/light** after a week of caring for one spot, **birdsong that steps forward** in the mix when a place heals, air that **feels easier to breathe**. Reference: *GRIS* end beat where life returns — **communication shorthand only**; **stylized vitality** (color, light, motion, sound layers), **not** magic singing or fantasy ecology. Still **believable** — one corner of a city path, not the whole map turning into Eden overnight.

**Example beat (voice for design):** Clean litter on **your** regular path for several days → return and the **grass beside the path** **lights up** — muted green → **alive green/gold** (tune so it’s not photosensitive-flashy). **Birdsong** **emphasized** — was thin or pigeon-only (Phase 3); now **one or two more layers**, slightly **louder/closer** in the mix when you pause. Player **feels** it before they **name** it. **No** infographic popup (“+12% biodiversity”).

**Plant beat (same system, faster clock):** You **saw the bud yesterday** — back from groceries and it’s **open**, same day or same week. *When did that happen?* — cozy surprise, not a quest checkbox. Neighbor thread may beat you to it with a photo.

**Examples (same system):**

- Healthier grass / cleaner paths — **visual read** first
- One or two birds visiting — **seen or heard**
- Fuller environmental sound — **mix emphasis**, not a stat
- A space that feels easier to breathe in — **palette + audio**, sparse thought optional

Nothing dramatic. The city is not fixed. The world is not saved.

But the protagonist realizes:

*This isn't fixing everything... but this place feels more alive now.*

This becomes the first proof that restoration works and that small actions can create meaningful **sensory** change — training the eye/ear for what people **normalize away** in real life.

*For later; animation needed* for grass-light / sound-layer polish beyond color shifts already planned for city vs retreat.

### Phase 6 — The beginning of restoration

The protagonist realizes that meaningful change does not have to start with massive systems.

It can begin locally.

The goal shifts from:

*How do I fix everything?*

to:

*How do I help this place become more alive?*

This becomes the beginning of the game's main journey:

- Understanding systems
- Restoring environments
- Maintaining improvements
- Helping positive changes spread outward over time

**Spreads outward (owner — pinned):** Small habits **ripple** through people who **see** them — not a lecture arc, not all at once. **Not always dialogue.** Phase 6 **alternates** three ripple types so **enough** of the arc is **observation and delayed recognition**, not a stream of compliments:

1. **Delayed mention** — They were **there** (beach, shared walk, café) and **said little or nothing then**. **Later**, another scene (you visit **their** garden, break room, thrift aisle): *I saw you planting those — I didn’t even know what they were, but look at the butterflies now.* They may **not** know “native” yet; they noticed **beauty and life** (e.g. milkweed → butterflies). Same pattern for bottle, litter, kid facts, packed lunch recipe — **past tense**, warm, specific.
2. **Quiet observation (no comment)** — Player **sees** the ripple without a speech: friend brings a bag, picks up one piece of trash, kid repeats a fact, a corner looks tended, more insects at someone’s pots. **Show;** optional one-line thought from protagonist, not NPC praise.
3. **Direct casual comment** — Immediate line from someone they know (*you always bring that bottle*) — **use sparingly**; not the default beat type.

**Mix target:** **Mostly delayed mention + quiet observation**, **some direct comment** — stagger across Phase 6; tie to Phase 5A habits the player has actually been doing. **Warm, low-key** — not eco-sainthood.

**What others usually notice vs not (owner — pinned):** Ripple beats target **observable** actions — **outdoor**, **workplace**, **café / public errands** (see **Phase 5A** lists). **Not** quiet home chores people rarely see or that would read like **cleaning-product advertising** — no ripple lines about **kitchen-sink habits**: dimming lights, unplugging, wash cold, compost, line-dry, **dishwater / gray water on plants**, **birdbath water on plants**, **soap blocks**, **detergent**, sponge brands, “switch to X soap,” etc. **Never** sound like an ad for detergents or the sink aisle. **Home → social exceptions (food & craft only):** (1) **Leftovers** — share a **recipe / meal tip** (*leftovers into chili pot pie — want the recipe?*). (2) **Crochet** with **all-natural / non-plastic fibers** — when **gifting or showing** a toy: *I like making these — I don’t like gifting plastic toys to kids* (delayed mention or direct comment when a kid receives one at beach, visit, etc.). **Not** observation of washing dishes at the sink. Window decals, nature clips on TV — still **no** default ripple.

**Pacing:** **One ripple beat per stretch** (not every habit at once). **Player alternation** OK — different runs get different beat **types** or lines for the same habit class (same flip idea as gorge / keepsake / 5A starters).

**City vs nature design philosophy (owner — pinned; game-wide from Phase 1):**

Cities and nature are **not** simple opposites. **Avoid:** *city bad, nature good* · *humans are the problem* · *civilization must disappear*. They are **different environments** with different pressures, limits, and restoration possibilities.

##### Cities are still living places

Cities stay **human and active** — work commutes, families, kids in cars, stores, neighbors talking, construction. **Not dead.** Environmental **life** is **diminished beneath** normal activity; that’s the gap, not humanity itself.

##### Nature creates contrast

Healthier natural areas help the protagonist **notice what’s missing** elsewhere — more alive, breathable, richer sound, softer light, emotionally relieving. **Not perfect utopias** — they **show** what greater environmental richness **can feel like** (Phase **2** retreat; Phase **3** return contrast).

##### Cities have different pressures

City choices are **context-shaped** — busy, convenience-heavy, time-pressed, noise drowning subtle natural sound, limited green space. People often make **worse environmental choices** because the **system around them makes better ones harder** (Phase **4** budget, greenwashing, commute foreshadow — **not** moral failure monologue).

##### Nature is not a magic solution

Natural areas can still have pollution, overuse, tourism pressure, invasives, restoration needs, human damage (retreat species lists include invasives where relevant). **Not** escape society forever — **understand** how different systems affect life.

##### City restoration should not erase the city

Improving the city **≠** turning it into wilderness. City **stays** a city. Restoration = healthier parks, cleaner rivers, more shade, more birds, better public spaces, **less harsh sensory overload** — **balance**, not replacement (Phase **5B** / **6** / **8+** vitality in **urban** spaces).

##### Different places need different solutions

Forest hikers vs office workers vs rural traditions vs city infrastructure — **context**, not one universal answer (Phase **5A** starters per place; Phase **8+** **positive momentum** without copy-paste fixes).

**Sensory restoration & environmental life sim (owner — pinned; implementation layer):**

**Goal:** An **environmental life sim** that **doesn’t clone real-life grind** — **felt and seen** more than **read** (no infographics, no dashboard sim). Help players **notice in real time** how much we **take for granted** — changes that are **actually amazing** once you **pay attention**.

**Design pillars:** **Environmental perception & soft horror** · **Audio restoration** · **Visual restoration** · **Sanctuary spaces**.

**Show, don’t tell (implementation hooks):**

- **Vitality read** — grass, leaves, water edge **gain light/warmth** when a place is cared for (5B / Phase 6 maintenance). See **visual restoration**.
- **Sound read** — birdsong thin → **layered**; pause on path = **hear the difference**. See **audio restoration**; Phase **3** baseline **less life, not just noise**.
- **Contrast with Phase 3** — muted/thin baseline makes **5B/6 payoff** legible without a lecture. **City vs nature:** contrast ≠ *city bad*; restoration = **more alive city**, not wilderness replacement.

**Realistic budget & resources (owner — pinned):**

- **Starts Phase 4** — ramps **gradually** through Phase 5 / 5A into Phase 6 (see **Budget awareness — starts Phase 4** under Phase 4).
- **Phase 4:** first **seen** money constraints — **organic grocery** can’t sustain whole week; optional **tagged** buys (native pot, grabber) **deferred** — **no lecture dialogue**. **Not** café bottle vs drink as budget beat.
- **Phase 5+:** constraints **visible** in errands/shopping more often; leftovers, affordable Phase 6 actions; **not** paying bills minigame hell.
- **Shown** through shelves, register, tags — **not** spreadsheet UI unless player **opts in** later; **not** forced dialogue beats.
- **Same realism guardrails** as 5A: no guilt for plastic week, no “ideal green citizen” scoring.
- **Not** The Sims workload — **enough** sim to reflect **budget vs wishful environmental goals**; **not** so much that play **is** real life.

**Timing — real-life aligned, fast + gradual mix (owner — pinned):**

- **Default cadence** stays **close to real life** — path care over **days**, grass vitality over **~a week**, seasonal plant arcs over **weeks** (not instant RPG trees).
- **Mix on purpose — keep them engaged:** Some payoffs land on **real-life quick clocks** (bud → bloom **same day or same week**, first butterfly, one bird layer returns). Some stay **gradual** (path grass **lighting up** over days, fuller soundscape over a week, neighbor thread filling in). **Alternate** fast surprises and slow burns so play doesn’t feel **all waiting** or **all instant**.
- **But real life is also fast:** a **bud** someone finally notices at the top can **bloom the same week** — sometimes **the same day** — before they realized it was ready. **Don’t drag every payoff** for game pacing; **some** wins **jump** while the player was at work or asleep.
- **Design beat:** return to a pot or path → *when did that open?* — neighbor chat ping (*did you see it?*), **vitality tick**, or **birdsong** bump **without** a cutscene announcing “Stage 3.” **Missed-it-then-noticed** is **valid** and **IRL-accurate**.
- Neighbor **growth photos** + **on-screen vitality** stay **in sync** — same plant, same angle; a **new frame** may show bloom **between** visits, not only on a schedule the player memorized.
- **Grass / litter path** = **gradual** band; **single-plant bloom / first visitor** = **quick** band — different scales, both honest.
- **Maintenance spectrum (Phase 8+):** Vitality and sound can **soften** when attention slips — **gradual**, not binary broken/fixed. **Stable care** lets a place **fully recover** and support **cross-place ripple** (migratory species, ecosystem knock-on — see **Maintenance instead of permanent victory**, 3H). **Player needed, not trapped** — no collapse if they skip a week.

**Fast vs gradual event tables (owner — proposed)** *(tuning in playtest)*:

- **Urban path / “litter band” (grass beside a walked route)**
  - **Quick surprises (1–2 in-game days):** first small green uptick in the cleaned strip; one noticeable insect sound; birdsong mix adds **one** extra layer for a stretch (or switches from pigeon/crow-only toward “one more bird type”).
  - **Gradual changes (~3–7 days):** grass shifts toward the “alive green/gold” vitality read; ambient soundscape feels thicker (more than just “birds louder,” but layered ambience); repeated returns make the change feel undeniable.
  - **Trigger hint:** quick events can fire after **enough** repeated care/clean passes (not after just one accidental pick-up).

- **Native pot / yard corner (one plant family you can track)**
  - **Quick surprises (same day to ~1 week):** bud opens; first butterfly/bee visit; new leaf emerges; the plant looks “different enough to notice” without needing a UI label.
  - **Gradual changes (~1–3 weeks):** fuller plant silhouette; more consistent visitor rhythm; neighbors’ photos show a clear “between visits” stage shift.
  - **Trigger hint:** quick surprises should feel like “I didn’t notice this until I looked” rather than an explosion cutscene.

- **Beach / tide edge (Newport)**
  - **Quick surprises (same day to ~1 week):** one added seabird layer in the sound mix; a tidier tide-pool/shoreline read after small trash removal; a more “alive” moment at the shoreline where the player pauses.
  - **Gradual changes (~1–2 weeks):** recurring bird presence; steadier ambient sound layers; visible shoreline vegetation/edge vitality improves without turning into a totally different ecosystem overnight.
  - **Trigger hint:** keep quick beats tied to what the player can plausibly affect (less debris, less obstruction, more safe habitat pockets).

- **Woods / cabin edge (Lake Tahoe)**
  - **Quick surprises (same day to ~1 week):** undergrowth “wakes up” visually; a single new bird type calls briefly; a noticeable increase in small insect ambience after the player cares for that edge.
  - **Gradual changes (~1–3 weeks):** richer layered soundscape; more consistent bird presence; repeated returns make the environment feel “steadier” and less muted.
  - **Trigger hint:** avoid constant bird noise; the improvement is “from thin to layered,” not “everywhere loud all the time.”

- **Gorge / river edge (Yosemite or Russian River)**
  - **Quick surprises (same week):** clearer “alive edge” read (less clutter, more visible life); one or two bird calls become more frequent; ambient water/edge sound gains clarity.
  - **Gradual changes (~2–4 weeks):** stabilized bank-vegetation read; more consistent wildlife “moments” when the player returns along the journal path.
  - **Trigger hint:** keep gorge changes intimate—small evidence the player learns to notice, not a dramatic ecosystem rebuild.

**Pacing tuning (engagement heuristics, owner — proposed)**:

- **Per return to a restored spot:** show at most **one** “quick visual” plus **one** “slow read” component (often slow read can be sound-layer emphasis).
- **Fast/slow mix target per week:** aim for **~30–45%** of returns to include a quick surprise once restoration is underway; the rest are gradual.
- **Guarantee for engagement:** once the player has hit at least **one** “5B-like care loop” for a spot, ensure they see **at least one** quick surprise in every **2–3 returns** to that same spot (varies by habitat scale).
- **Birdsong emphasis rule:** after Phase 3’s baseline of thin/pigeon-only birdsong, birdsong should become **audibly layered** at least every **other** return during Phase 5B → Phase 6, with “too much” avoided (no constant birdwall).
- **Missed-it-then-noticed is valid:** allow quick events to appear “between visits” via neighbor photos/growth frames; when the player returns, the surprise is “oh it already opened,” not “a cutscene begins.”
