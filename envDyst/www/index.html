The same morning as always

Traffic hums. Someone waters the sidewalk trees. The air carries coffee, diesel, and cut grass—layered, ordinary, full.

Sound feels thick here: voices overlap, a dog barks two blocks away, a door closes with weight.

Nothing announces a villain. The world is not on fire. It is simply complete in the way a lived place can be.

You see the same streets

People still commute. Sprinklers still tick. But color sits a step back, like a photograph left in a window. Sound arrives thinner—present, yet less generous.

This is not a future shock. It is a present-day dystopia felt through perception: systems you did not notice now shape what feels alive.

Small, sustained actions—understanding connections, restoring pockets of care—bring richness back: clearer sound, calmer spaces, color that remembers how to deepen.
